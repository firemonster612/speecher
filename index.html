01 · Capture

One shortcut, any app

Press your shortcut and the overlay appears over whatever you're doing: your editor, a browser field, a chat box. Toggle it, or hold the key and talk, push-to-talk style.

On macOS Speecher registers its own global hotkey. On Linux the compositor owns shortcuts, so you bind one key to speecher toggle and you're done.

02 · Context

It knows where you're typing

When a dictation starts, Speecher reads the focused app and control through AT-SPI on Linux and the Accessibility API on macOS: which app, which field, the caret, a little of the surrounding text.

From the target it infers a writing profile, so a Slack message, a work email, and a commit message don't come out sounding the same. Cleanup lands in the register the destination calls for.

03 · Speak edits

Edit out loud

Corrections you speak are applied before the text lands, then removed. The final text carries only the fix.

Technical dictation stays literal: spoken symbols become real characters, and commands, paths, and identifiers come out in backticks.

"deploy it to production… I meant staging, not production"
becomes
Deploy it to staging.
"git push origin slash fix dash overlay"
becomes
git push origin/fix-overlay

04 · Learn

It learns how you talk

Vocabulary entries keep names and jargon spelled your way. Snippets expand a short spoken trigger into text you wrote once.

And when you correct a word right after Speecher delivers it, that fix is learned locally. Fixing "at spy" to "AT-SPI" only ever happens once.
