Talk instead of type.

Speecher is an open-source dictation app for Linux and macOS. Hit a shortcut, say what you mean, and clean text lands in the app you were already using.

Download for Linux Download for macOS View source
Free, MIT licensed. One AppImage on Linux, a DMG on Apple silicon.

Runs on the subscription you already have. Not a new one.

Sign in with Claude or ChatGPT and you're done. Speech recognition goes through Claude's own voice stack or your ChatGPT account via Codex, and the text cleanup runs on those same logins. No per-minute metering, no markup, no credit packs.

Prefer an API key? That works too. Either way your audio travels from your machine straight to your provider. There is no Speecher server in the middle, and nothing here costs money.

Dictate into anything

One shortcut, any app

Press your Global Shortcut and start talking. The overlay shows words as you say them, playing media pauses until you finish, and the text is delivered into whatever had focus.

Delivery is app-aware: per-application paste rules decide whether text is typed in, placed on the clipboard, or held back, and Speecher watches the focused control (via AT-SPI on Linux) so it knows what it's typing into. On Plasma the shortcut registers itself; GNOME and Hyprland go through the desktop's own shortcut dialog.

Cleanup with context

It knows where you're typing

Raw transcripts ramble. Speecher can pass them through Claude or GPT with a Writing Profile inferred from the target app: Work, Email, Personal, AI coding, or your own override for a specific application. A Slack message and a commit message shouldn't read the same, and they won't.

You pick the model and the cleanup strength per profile, and it can use the target window (even a screenshot of it, if you allow that) as context for better rewrites.

Your words

It learns how you talk

Add the jargon, names, and project words that generic speech models fumble, and star the ones that matter. When you fix a transcript right after it lands, Speecher notices the edit and learns the correction on its own.

Spoken snippets expand into text you wrote once and never want to dictate again, and replacement rules fix the things a model keeps getting wrong.

Low-maintenance

Updates that ask first

Two Update Channels: Stable Release, or a Nightly Build republished from every push to master. By default Speecher only tells you an update exists. One click installs it. A separate toggle exists for people who want silent background updates, and it's off until you say so.

The Linux AppImage replaces itself in place; macOS updates ride Sparkle and are verified against the project's signing key. After an update, a What's New page shows the release notes next to the new settings themselves, live, so you can try them on the spot.

A real desktop app

Native, not a browser tab

On Linux it's a Qt app that follows your color scheme and feels at home on KDE Plasma. On macOS the settings are native SwiftUI. Same features on both, no Electron anywhere.

Everything you saw above is one window with a search box. The update channel, the writing profiles, the paste rules: all of it is a setting you can find, not a config file you have to hunt for.

Get Speecher

Linux

One AppImage for x86_64. Make it executable and run it; it updates itself from then on and carries zsync metadata for external update tools. Wayland desktops; happiest on KDE Plasma.

Download AppImage

macOS

A DMG for Apple silicon, macOS 26 and later. The app is unsigned (there's no $99 Apple subscription behind this project either), so the first launch needs one trip to System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway. Updates are painless after that.
