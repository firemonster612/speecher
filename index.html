Sign in with Claude or ChatGPT and you're done. Speech recognition runs through Claude's own voice stack or your ChatGPT account via Codex, and the text cleanup uses those same logins.

Your audio travels from your machine straight to your provider. There's no Speecher server in the middle, nothing is metered, and nothing here costs money.

01 · CAPTURE

One shortcut, any app

Press your Global Shortcut and start talking. The overlay shows words as you say them, playing media pauses until you finish, and the text is delivered into whatever had focus.

Delivery is app-aware: per-application paste rules decide whether text is typed in, placed on the clipboard, or held back, and Speecher watches the focused control (AT-SPI on Linux, the Accessibility API on macOS) so it knows exactly what it's typing into.

02 · REFINE

Cleanup that knows where you're typing

Raw transcripts ramble, like the struck-through line you watched get rewritten above. Speecher can pass them through Claude or GPT with a Writing Profile inferred from the target app (Work, Email, Personal, AI coding) or your own override for a specific application. A Slack message and a commit message shouldn't read the same, and they won't.

You pick the model and cleanup strength per profile, and it can use the target window (even a screenshot of it, if you allow that) as context.

03 · LEARN

It learns how you talk

Add the jargon, names, and project words that generic speech models fumble, and star the ones that matter. When you fix a transcript right after it lands, Speecher notices and learns the correction on its own — at spy AT‑SPI only ever happens once.

Spoken snippets expand into text you wrote once and never want to dictate again, and replacement rules fix the things a model keeps getting wrong.

04 · UPDATE

Updates that ask first

Two Update Channels: Stable Release, or a Nightly Build republished from every push to master. By default Speecher only tells you an update exists. One click installs it. The silent-background-updates toggle exists, and it's off until you say otherwise.

The Linux AppImage replaces itself in place; macOS updates ride Sparkle. After an update, a What's New page shows the release notes next to the new settings themselves, live, so you can try them on the spot.

05 · NATIVE

Native on your desktop

On Linux it's a real Qt app that follows your color scheme and feels at home on KDE Plasma. On macOS the settings are native SwiftUI, not a port and not a web view. Same features on both, no Electron anywhere.

Everything above is one window with a search box. The update channel, the writing profiles, the paste rules: all of it is a setting you can find, not a config file you have to hunt for.
